**You'll need:** Ollama with `nomic-embed-text`, `d1-omni:600m` and `llama3.2:3b`. This lesson is its own small project; Lesson
2.3 introduces the decision model.

A vector search returns the passages closest to the question, and close isn't the same as useful. Module 4 sent the top-K
straight into the prompt, so an off-topic passage, an outdated policy or a passage someone planted with instructions for the
assistant reached the model with the rest. Spring AI 2.1 adds two document post-processors that ask a decision model about each
passage before the prompt is built: `DecisionDocumentFilter` drops passages, and `DecisionDocumentReranker` orders what's left.
This lesson puts both into a RAG pipeline, measures what they do with a small local model, and sets them up from what we
measured.

## 1. Spring mental model

A `DocumentPostProcessor` runs after retrieval and before augmentation, like a servlet filter between the repository and the
view. `RetrievalAugmentationAdvisor.documentPostProcessors(...)` runs them in the order you give.

<Mermaid
  caption="The lesson's pipeline. Each post-processor makes one decision-model call per passage."
  code={`
flowchart LR
  Q["Question"] --> V["Vector search<br/>top 6 by similarity"]
  V --> F["DecisionDocumentFilter<br/>4 questions per passage"]
  F -->|"INCLUDED, CONFLICTING"| R["DecisionDocumentReranker<br/>answers_query score"]
  F -.->|"EXCLUDED"| X["dropped"]
  R -->|"top 3, score ≥ 0.4"| P["Prompt"]
`}
/>

The filter asks four yes/no questions about each passage, over a `FieldsInput` that holds the question and the passage. It
applies its `Policy` in a fixed order, and the first rule that matches decides:

| Order | Question | Rule (`Policy.defaults()`) | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `contains_prompt_injection` | above 0.7 | `EXCLUDED` |
| 2 | `contradicts_query_premise` | above 0.7 | kept as `CONFLICTING` |
| 3 | `is_relevant` | below 0.45 | `EXCLUDED` |
| 4 | `contains_answer_evidence` | above 0.55 | `INCLUDED`, otherwise `EXCLUDED` |

These rules come from the 2.1.0 classes and from running them:

- Every comparison is strict: a passage with evidence exactly 0.55 is excluded. (`isYes(threshold)` in Lesson 2.3 is inclusive.)
- A kept passage carries its class in the metadata key `decision.classification`. A `CONFLICTING` passage is kept, and the
  contradiction check comes before relevance, so an unrelated passage that "contradicts" the question survives.
- The reranker asks one question, `answers_query`, stores the probability under `decision.rerank.score` and sorts by it. By
  default it keeps every passage (no `topK`, `minimumScore` 0).
- Both run four passages at a time and allow one minute for the whole batch. When the minute runs out, the filter logs
  `Could not screen N of M documents, passing them through unscreened` and keeps them, unless you set `failClosed(true)`.
  The reranker has no such switch: it keeps the passages it couldn't score after the scored ones, without a score and without
  the `minimumScore` check, up to `topK`.

- Pick the dishwasher question and press **Spring AI defaults**. All six retrieved passages reach the prompt, both planted ones
  among them: `d1-omni:600m` rated nearly every unrelated passage above 0.7 for "contradicts the question", so the filter kept
  them as `CONFLICTING`.
- Switch to **This lesson**: contradiction at 1.00 (the check never fires), a reranker minimum score of 0.4 and a top 3. Each

- Pick the address question. Its right passage scored 0.173 for evidence and the planted one 0.208, so no evidence threshold keeps

- Lower the injection threshold until a planted passage is dropped for injection. The right address passage (0.565) goes first.

## 4. Production gotchas & security

<Gotcha kind="reliability" title="Measure the filter before you trust it">
With `Policy.defaults()` and `d1-omni:600m`, the filter kept five of six retrieved passages for the return question and six of six
for the dishwasher question, almost all as `CONFLICTING`. The policy is a set of thresholds on someone else's model. Label a few
dozen question-and-passage pairs from your own knowledge base, run them through the filter, and set the policy from what it keeps.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha kind="security" title="A timeout passes passages through unscreened">
The one-minute timeout covers all the passages of a question. On a slow machine or a busy model it runs out, and by default the
passages that weren't judged go into the prompt as they are, a planted one included. Set `failClosed(true)` and a timeout you have
measured: screening six passages took about 8 seconds here, filter and reranker together. A reranker timeout is milder, because
its passages were already screened, but they arrive without a score, so code that reads `decision.rerank.score` must allow null.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha kind="security" title="The injection question missed both planted passages">
`d1-omni:600m` scored the planted return passage 0.223 and the planted address passage 0.320 for injection, and the genuine address
passage 0.565. The filter dropped the planted ones for low evidence, not as injections. Keep instructions out of the knowledge base
when you ingest it (Module 4), and make tools check who may do what (Module 5): what reaches the prompt is still untrusted text.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha kind="reliability" title="These settings were tuned on three questions">
A contradiction threshold of 1.0 and a minimum score of 0.4 give the right result for the three recorded questions, and they cost
one: the address question loses its right passage and gets "can't answer". Turning `CONFLICTING` off also loses a useful label:
the outdated final-sale passage now reaches the prompt as `INCLUDED` when it is ranked high enough. Fix that one in the knowledge
base, and re-measure the policy on more questions before you rely on it.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha kind="cost" title="One call per passage, per post-processor">
Six passages cost six filter calls of four questions each, then six reranker calls: about 6 and 2 seconds on this machine's CPU.
Four calls at a time made no difference, because Ollama answered one request at a time. The 27B model behind the sandbox's labels
took 6 to 47 seconds per passage, which is why it is the reference here, not the pipeline.
</Gotcha>

## Recap

- `DecisionDocumentFilter` and `DecisionDocumentReranker` are `DocumentPostProcessor`s: add them to
  `RetrievalAugmentationAdvisor.documentPostProcessors(...)`, filter first.
- The filter checks injection, contradiction, relevance and evidence in that order, with strict thresholds; `CONFLICTING`
  passages are kept.
- The reranker scores `answers_query` and keeps everything unless you set `topK` and `minimumScore`.
- Set `failClosed(true)`: by default a timeout lets unscreened passages through.
- Measure on your own labelled pairs. With `d1-omni:600m` the defaults kept nearly every passage, and the injection question
  caught neither planted one.
